## What is Gratuity?

Gratuity is a lump-sum payment made by your employer when you leave after serving
at least **5 continuous years**. It is your legal right under the Payment of
Gratuity Act 1972.

## Who is eligible?

- Any employee who has worked for 5 or more years in the same establishment
- Applies to: factories, mines, oilfields, plantations, ports, railways, shops,
  and any establishment with 10 or more employees
- Also payable on death or disablement (5-year rule waived)

## How is it calculated?

$$\text{Gratuity} = \frac{\text{Last drawn monthly salary} \times 15 \times \text{years of service}}{26}$$

- "Last drawn salary" = basic salary + dearness allowance
- Each completed year counts; a period of more than 6 months in the last year
  counts as a full year
- Maximum ceiling: ₹20 lakh (as of 2024)

## What should your employer do?

- Pay within **30 days** of the date gratuity becomes payable
- If not paid within 30 days, simple interest at the rate notified by the
  government accrues from the due date

## What to do if gratuity is denied?

1. Send a written demand notice to your employer citing Section 4, PG Act 1972
2. If no response within 30 days → file an application with the **Controlling
   Authority** (Labour Commissioner in your district) — Form I
3. The process is **free** and does not require a lawyer
4. You can also contact the NALSA helpline: **1516**

## Important

This information is general. Gratuity rules may vary slightly for government
employees. Verify with a qualified professional for large amounts.
