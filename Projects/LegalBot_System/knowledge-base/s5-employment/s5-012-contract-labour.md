## What is Contract Labour?

Contract labour refers to workers hired through a **contractor** (manpower agency,
third-party vendor) to work on the premises of a principal employer (the company
where the actual work happens). Common in security, housekeeping, IT outsourcing,
logistics, and manufacturing.

## Key Rights of Contract Workers

### Wages Must Equal Direct Employees

If a contract worker performs the same or similar work as a regular employee of
the principal employer, they are entitled to the **same wages and service conditions**
(Section 21).

### Amenities the Contractor Must Provide (Section 16–19)

- Canteen (if 100+ workers)
- Rest rooms
- Drinking water
- First aid
- Sufficient number of latrines / urinals

### If Contractor Defaults — Principal Employer is Liable

If the contractor fails to pay wages or provide amenities, the **principal
employer is directly liable** to make the payment (Section 20–21). The principal
employer can then recover the amount from the contractor.

## Regularisation of Contract Workers

- Contract labour can be **abolished** by government notification in perennial or
  core activities
- Workers whose contracts are abolished may have a right to regularisation as
  per Supreme Court judgements (SAIL case guidelines)

## What to Do if Wages are Unpaid or Conditions are Poor

1. Complain to both the contractor and the principal employer in writing
2. File with the **Registering Officer** under the CLRA Act (Labour Commissioner)
3. NALSA: **1516**
