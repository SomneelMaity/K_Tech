## What is Retrenchment?

Retrenchment means termination of employment by the employer for any reason other
than disciplinary action (not retirement, resignation, or health-based termination).
It is governed by the Industrial Disputes Act 1947.

## Mandatory Conditions Before Retrenchment (Section 25F)

An employer **cannot** retrench a worker unless:
1. The worker has been given **one month's written notice** (or wages in lieu)
2. The worker has been paid **retrenchment compensation** = 15 days' average pay
   for every completed year of continuous service
3. **Prior permission** is obtained from the appropriate government authority if
   the establishment has 100 or more workers (Section 25N)

## Order of Retrenchment (Section 25G)

- "Last in, first out" rule must be followed within the same category/grade
- Employer must give reasons if this order is not followed

## Right to Re-employment (Section 25H)

- If the employer re-hires for the same or similar post within 1 year, retrenched
  workers must be given **preference**

## What to Do if Retrenched Illegally

1. File a complaint at the **Labour Commissioner's office** in your district
2. Alternatively, raise an **Industrial Dispute** under Section 2A — individual
   disputes on discharge/dismissal are now maintainable directly
3. Time limit: Raise dispute within **3 years**
4. Free legal aid: NALSA **1516**

## Important Notes

- These protections apply to "workmen" (non-managerial roles earning below a
  notified wage ceiling)
- Managers and supervisory staff have weaker statutory protections; their remedies
  are via civil courts
