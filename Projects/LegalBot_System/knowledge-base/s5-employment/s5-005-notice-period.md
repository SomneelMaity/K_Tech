## Notice Period — General Rule

For establishments covered under the Industrial Employment (Standing Orders) Act
1946, the certified standing orders specify the notice period. Typical minimums:

| Employment Category | Notice Period |
|--------------------|---------------|
| Permanent worker   | 30 days (common) |
| Probationer        | None or 1–7 days |
| Casual worker      | None required |

## Pay in Lieu of Notice

If an employer terminates without serving the full notice period, they must pay
**wages for the balance notice period**. Similarly, if you resign without serving
notice, you may owe your employer wages for the shortfall — but only if the
standing orders or your contract say so.

## Establishments with 100+ Workers (Section 25N, ID Act)

- 90 days' written notice required before retrenchment
- **Prior government permission** mandatory
- If government does not respond within 60 days → permission deemed granted

## What Your Employer Must Give at Termination

- Full and final settlement of salary, unpaid leave encashment, and bonus within
  a reasonable time (typically 30–45 days)
- **Service certificate** — you can demand this; refusal is an unfair labour practice
- **PF transfer/withdrawal forms** — employer must process within 5 days of request

## What to Do if Notice Pay is Withheld

1. Send a written demand to HR citing your standing orders or appointment letter
2. File a complaint with the **Assistant Labour Commissioner** if no response
   within 30 days
3. NALSA helpline: **1516**
