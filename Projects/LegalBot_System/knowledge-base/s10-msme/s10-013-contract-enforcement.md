## What Makes a Contract Valid? (Section 10)

A contract is valid when it has:
1. **Offer and acceptance** — clear terms agreed by both parties
2. **Consideration** — something of value exchanged (money, service, goods)
3. **Free consent** — no coercion, undue influence, fraud, or misrepresentation
4. **Competent parties** — both parties are adults of sound mind
5. **Lawful object** — not forbidden by law

## NDAs (Non-Disclosure Agreements)

NDAs are valid and enforceable in India under the Contract Act. Key points:
- Must clearly define what constitutes "confidential information"
- Should specify the **duration** of the obligation
- Remedy on breach: **injunction** (court order to stop disclosure) + damages

## Non-Compete Clauses (Section 27)

**Post-employment/post-contract non-compete clauses are generally void** in India
(Section 27 — restraint of trade). Courts will not enforce a clause that prevents
a former employee from working in their field after leaving.

**Exception**: Clauses restricting a partner from using firm trade secrets or
soliciting firm clients for a **reasonable period** may be enforced in specific
business sale contexts.

## Breach of Contract — Remedies (Section 73–74)

- **Actual damages** (Section 73): Compensation for actual loss suffered
- **Liquidated damages / penalty** (Section 74): If the contract specifies a sum
  for breach, courts award that sum or actual damages, **whichever is reasonable**
  — Indian courts can reduce an excessive penalty clause
- **Specific performance**: Court orders the party to perform the contract
  (available through Civil Court)

## Enforcing a Contract

1. Send a formal **Legal Notice** (can be sent by you or a lawyer)
2. If no response within 15–30 days → file civil suit in District Court
3. For quick interim relief (stopping ongoing breach) → apply for **interim injunction**
4. Alternatively, invoke the **arbitration clause** if the contract has one
5. NALSA for free legal aid: **1516**
