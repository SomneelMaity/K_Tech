## When Does an MSME Face Insolvency?

An MSME may face insolvency proceedings if:
- It **defaults on a debt** of ₹1 lakh or more (threshold for MSME — lower than
  ₹1 crore threshold for large corporates, raised in 2020 COVID notification, but
  MSME threshold remains lower)
- A **financial creditor** (bank) or **operational creditor** (supplier, employee)
  files an application at NCLT (National Company Law Tribunal)

## Pre-Packaged Insolvency Resolution Process (PPIRP) — MSME Specific

Introduced in 2021, PPIRP allows an MSME to resolve insolvency **faster and
confidentially** without full CIRP:

- Initiated by the **corporate debtor** (MSME itself) with approval of 66%
  unrelated financial creditors
- MSME promoters can submit a **base resolution plan** to retain control
- NCLT must admit or reject within **30 days** (vs 14 days for CIRP)
- Resolution plan must be approved within **90 days** (extendable to 90 more)
- Much cheaper and less disruptive than standard CIRP

## Standard CIRP (for larger defaults)

If PPIRP is not available or fails → Corporate Insolvency Resolution Process:
- Resolution professional takes over management
- Committee of Creditors formed
- Resolution plan within 330 days
- If no plan → liquidation

## Protection for MSME Promoters (Section 29A exception)

MSME promoters are **exempt from Section 29A** — they can bid for their own
company during CIRP/liquidation, which large company promoters cannot.

## Personal Guarantors (Banks)

If you gave a personal guarantee for the MSME's bank loan, the bank can
separately initiate insolvency against you personally under Part III of IBC.
