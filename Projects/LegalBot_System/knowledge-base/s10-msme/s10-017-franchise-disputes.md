## What is a Franchise Agreement?

A franchise agreement gives a **franchisee** the right to use the **franchisor's**
brand, business model, and systems in exchange for fees/royalties. India has no
specific franchise law — these contracts are governed by the **Indian Contract Act
1872** and general principles of contract law.

## Common Disputes

- Abrupt or wrongful **termination** of franchise without proper notice
- Non-disclosure of key information before signing (misrepresentation)
- Excessive territorial encroachment by the franchisor or other franchisees
- Disputes over royalty calculation or audit rights
- Renewal refusals

## Key Rights of a Franchisee

- Right to receive **accurate disclosure** of business performance, fees, and
  obligations before signing
- Right to the **agreed exclusive territory** (if mentioned in the contract)
- Right to **proper notice** before termination (as specified in the contract)
- Right to compensation for losses if termination is wrongful (Section 73)

## Anti-competitive Clauses (Competition Act 2002)

- A franchisor cannot impose **resale price maintenance** (fixing the minimum
  price you must charge customers) — this is a per se violation of Section 3(4)
- Unreasonable exclusive supply or exclusive dealing restrictions can be
  challenged before the **Competition Commission of India (CCI)**

## Dispute Resolution

1. Check the franchise agreement's **dispute resolution clause** — most have
   arbitration clauses (Arbitration & Conciliation Act 1996)
2. If arbitration is provided → initiate arbitration (cost-effective, faster)
3. If no clause → file civil suit in the court with agreed jurisdiction
4. For CCI complaints: **competition.nic.in** (free to file)

## Practical Steps Before Signing

- Insist on a **Franchise Disclosure Document (FDD)** — India has no mandatory
  FDD requirement, but reputable franchisors provide one
- Have a lawyer review before signing
- Negotiate exit clauses carefully
