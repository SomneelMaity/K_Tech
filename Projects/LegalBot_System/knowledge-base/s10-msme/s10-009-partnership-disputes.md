## Key Rights of a Partner (Section 12)

Every partner has the right to:
- Participate in the **management of the firm**
- Inspect and copy the **firm's books of accounts**
- Share in the **profits** (equally if partnership deed is silent)
- Receive interest on capital/loans at the rate specified in the deed
- **Not be expelled** except under a power expressly given in the partnership deed

## If a Partner is Wrongfully Excluded

- File a suit in the **Civil Court** for declaration that the exclusion is void
- Apply for injunction to prevent disposal of firm assets
- Accounts can be ordered by the court

## When Can a Partner Leave? (Section 32)

- **Express will / retirement**: Give notice as per the deed; if deed is silent,
  give reasonable written notice to all other partners
- **Dissolution by agreement**: All partners agree in writing
- **Dissolution by court** (Section 44): Court can order dissolution if a partner
  becomes of unsound mind, is guilty of misconduct, or the firm is being run at a
  loss that makes it just and equitable to dissolve

## Dissolution and Winding Up (Section 39–45)

On dissolution:
1. Firm's **liabilities are paid first**: third parties → partners' loans → partners'
   capital → remaining profits distributed in profit-sharing ratio
2. Each partner is entitled to have the **partnership accounts settled** and to
   receive their share from the proceeds

## LLP vs Partnership

If the business is an LLP (Limited Liability Partnership), the LLP Act 2008 governs.
Partners have limited liability and disputes go through LLP agreement provisions
first, then NCLT.

## Practical Steps for Disputes

1. Refer to the **Partnership Deed** — most disputes are resolved by its terms
2. If no deed or deed is silent → Indian Partnership Act 1932 default rules apply
3. File a **Civil Suit** in the District Court with jurisdiction over the firm's location
4. Consider **Mediation / Arbitration** if the deed has an arbitration clause
5. NALSA: **1516**
