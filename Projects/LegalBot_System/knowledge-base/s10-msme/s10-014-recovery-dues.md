## When to Use a Civil Suit for Recovery?

When a debtor (client, customer, buyer) owes you a specific sum of money and is
not paying despite repeated reminders, you can file a **money recovery suit** in
a Civil Court.

## Order 37 — Summary Suit (Fastest Route)

For recovery of:
- Money due on a **negotiable instrument** (cheque, promissory note, bill of exchange)
- Money due on a **written contract** with an ascertained sum

Under Order 37, the defendant has **limited defences** and must apply for
leave to defend within 10 days. If no leave is obtained, a **decree is granted
immediately** without full trial.

## Which Court Has Jurisdiction?

| Claim Amount | Court |
|-------------|-------|
| Up to ₹20 lakh (approx.) | Civil Judge (Junior Division) |
| ₹20 lakh – ₹1 crore | Civil Judge (Senior Division) / District Court |
| Above ₹1 crore | High Court (Original Side, where available) |

Jurisdiction: sue in the court where the **defendant resides** OR where the
**contract was to be performed** or breach occurred.

## Court Fee (Approximate)

Approximately **1–5% of the claim amount** (varies by state). This is recoverable
from the losing party when you win.

## Steps

1. Send a **legal notice** (creates evidence of demand and date of default)
2. If unpaid after 15–30 days → file plaint with court fee and supporting documents
   (invoices, contracts, bank statements, delivery records)
3. Court issues summons to defendant
4. For Order 37: if defendant does not seek leave within 10 days → decree in your favour
5. Execute the decree (bank attachment, property attachment) to recover the amount

## Alternative: Lok Adalat

For amounts up to ₹1 crore, consider the **Permanent Lok Adalat** — quick, free,
and binding if both parties agree. Contact your District Legal Services Authority.
