## What is Section 138?

If a cheque given for payment of a debt or liability is dishonoured by the bank,
the issuer commits a criminal offence under **NI Act Section 138**.
Punishment: imprisonment up to 2 years and/or fine up to 2× the cheque amount.

## Strict Time-Lines — Do Not Miss These

| Step | Deadline |
|------|----------|
| Receive bank dishonour memo | Day 0 |
| Send written demand notice | Within **30 days** of bank memo |
| Waiting period for payment | 15 days from notice receipt |
| File criminal complaint in Magistrate's Court | Within **30 days** after 15-day period ends |

**Missing any deadline kills the criminal case.**

## What the Demand Notice Must Contain

- Your name and address
- Cheque number, date, amount, and issuing bank
- Date of dishonour and reason given by bank
- Demand for payment within 15 days of receipt
- Statement that criminal action will follow if unpaid

Send by **registered post with acknowledgement due** (AD). Keep the original
returned cheque, bank memo, and proof of notice delivery.

## What to do

1. Get the bank memo (dishonour slip) the day the cheque bounces
2. Draft and send the demand notice within 30 days — do not delay
3. If unpaid after 15 days → file complaint in the Judicial Magistrate's Court
   (First Class) where the cheque was presented
4. Attach: original cheque, bank memo, copy of notice, postal AD card

## Civil Recovery in Parallel

You can also file a civil suit for recovery of the cheque amount independently.
Many creditors do both simultaneously.
